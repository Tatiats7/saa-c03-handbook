# CONVENTIONS: the authoring contract (version 1.0)

Every writer and every checking script follows this file. `RULINGS.md` overrides it. `SPEC.json` holds every number in this file as data; when the two differ, `SPEC.json` wins and this file is wrong. `STYLE.md` holds the writing rules. `CONVENTIONS-2.md` is part 2 of this file, with the same authority: pattern, front and synthesis pages, the rules for JSON items, and the additions to `STYLE.md`. `STARTUP.md` is the fact sheet of the startup, Callvale. A complete miniature chapter that follows this contract is in `aws-saa-book/tools/tests/fixtures/book/`.

Revision: 1.0, frozen 2026-10-11 after the exemplar panel and its fixes (`_build/reports/exemplar-fix.md`); slice: `_build/style/exemplar/`; additions: `CONVENTIONS-2.md` U.

Paths are relative to `aws-saa-book/`. A chapter folder is `<NN>-<slug>/`, with id and slug from `data/book.json` (seed: `_build/registry/book.seed.json`).

The template key is `kind` when the registry entry has one, else `tier`. File numbers keep their meaning in every folder: 1 fast, 2 depth, 3 do, 4 say, 5 quiz. The tool also generates `facts/<service>.md` and `glossary.md`. ## B. Front matter

A restricted YAML block between two `---` lines. One key per line. A value is a scalar, a flow list `[a, b]` or a flow map `{k: v}`. No multi-line values. Put double quotes around digits-only strings and around strings that contain a colon.

`1-fast.md` carries the chapter metadata:

```yaml
---
id: "10"
slug: vpc
title: Amazon VPC
file: fast
tier: deep
xl: true
domains: [1, 2, 3]
tasks: ["1.2", "2.2", "3.4"]
services: [vpc]
minutes: {fast: 20, pass: 65, full: 150}
status: draft
updated: 2026-10-06
---
```

`id`, `slug`, `title`, `tier`, `services` (and `kind`, `xl` when present) equal the registry entry. `tasks` are exam-guide task statements. `minutes` are whole minutes for the Fast, Pass and Full routes: `fast` is the sum of the H2 time boxes of `1-fast.md` (checked); `pass` and `full` follow `lint.minutes_formula` in `SPEC.json` (the lint report prints the computed values; copy them). The template `minutes` in `SPEC.json` are planning targets, not rules. `status` is `planned`, `draft`, `reviewed` or `ready`: write `draft`; after that only the tool changes it (`stage end`).

Every other hand-written file:

```yaml
---
chapter: "10"
file: deep
title: Amazon VPC in depth
status: draft
updated: 2026-10-06
---
```

`file` is `deep`, `services`, `calendar` or `mistakes`. Each file has exactly one H1, equal to its `title`.

## C. Sections

Each H2 is a section with exact heading text, a time box, and a flag comment on the next line:

```markdown
## Signals and traps (4 min)
<!-- section: role=signals; route=fast; minutes=4 -->
```

- Flag syntax: `<!-- section: role=<role>; route=<fast|pass|full>; minutes=<n> -->`, then a blank line. `minutes` equals the time box in the heading. In `1-fast.md` the H2 time boxes add up to `minutes.fast`; the H3 time boxes under "How it works" add up to its H2 time box.
- Sections appear in the order below. Required sections cannot be left out. No other H2 is allowed.
- `route` says which route first includes the section. An H3 under "How it works" is marked for the Pass route with `<!-- section: route=pass -->` on the line after it. Mark 1 to 4 subsections: the ones that the core questions review.
- A section holds only what its `counts` and `fields` in `SPEC.json` name. A role decides the fact rule (section F), never the devices.
- A section id (the heading anchor without the time box, section G) is unique in the chapter folder.

**`1-fast.md` of a deep or medium chapter** (all route `fast`):

| # | H2 text | role | Contents (deep / medium) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Predict` | `predict` | 3 / 2 Predict prompts, one gist trap |
| 2 | `Look first` | `task` | one or two sentences and one Look task region (optional in medium) |
| 3 | `In one breath` | `breath` | one-breath definition, the problem, Predict the picture with the hero diagram, startup example |
| 4 | `Words that go together` | `words` | words table, 4 to 8 / 3 to 5 rows |
| 5 | `Choose it or not` | `choose` | 2 to 4 / 1 to 2 comparison tables |
| 6 | `Signals and traps` | `signals` | signals table 6 to 8 / 6 rows; `### Traps` list 4 to 5 / 3 |
| 7 | `Changed recently` | `changed` | 0 to 4 entries |
| 8 | `Check` | `check` | 3 / 2 Check prompts, parallel to Predict |
| 9 | `Recap from memory` | `recap` | 3 / 3 Recap prompts |

**`2-deep.md` of a deep or medium chapter** (all route `full`):

| # | H2 text | role | Contents (deep / medium) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `How it works` | `how` | 8 to 14 / 4 to 7 subsections |
| 2 | `Numbers that decide answers` | `numbers` | `### A-list` (at most 8 / 5 rows), `### B-list` |
| 3 | `Decision record` | `example` | the startup's decision record |
| 4 | `FAQ quick-fire` | `faq` | 10 / 5 FAQ items |
| 5 | `In the real world` | `realworld` | optional; one case box |

**Light family.** `1-fast.md`: `The family in one picture` (`map`), `Confusable pairs` (`choose`, one `pairs` table), `Signals and traps` (`signals`), `Check` (`check`), `Recap from memory` (`recap`). `2-services.md`: one H2 per service, heading equal to the official service name, role `card`, with a `service=` key (section D15).

**Pattern.** `1-fast.md`: `Predict`, `The question behind it` (`question`), `The spectrum` (`map`, the hero diagram), `Patterns at a glance` (`glance`, one `glance` table), `Service by service` (`matrix`, one `services` table), `Signals and traps`, `Check`, `Recap from memory`. `2-deep.md`: `The patterns` (`how`; 6 to 10 pattern cards as H3 subsections), `Numbers that decide answers` (optional), `Decision record`. Details and examples: `CONVENTIONS-2.md` section P.

**Front, synthesis, mock.** The H2 list of each file is in `SPEC.json` under `chapter_overrides` (00, 01, 44 to 49, 52) and `templates.mock`. Their page forms are in `CONVENTIONS-2.md` section S.

Role decides how strict the fact rule is (section F): `example`, `task`, `worked` and `predict` allow story values; `orient`, `method` and `plan` are advice about the exam or about studying (heuristic text); every other role is explanatory and strict.

## D. Devices

Three building blocks are used by many devices.

**Prompt.** A paragraph that starts with one of the seven bold prompt labels of `SPEC.json` `prompts.labels`: `**Predict 1.**`, `**Check 1.**`, `**Recap 1.**`, `**FAQ 1.**`, `**Stop and answer.**`, `**Spot the bug.**`, `**Predict the picture.**`. Only a prompt is an example block: its scenario can use story values.

**Reveal.** Exactly this shape, with the blank lines:

```markdown
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. A shelf stores widgets in [one AWS Region](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-scope).

</details>
```

Allowed summaries: `Answer`, `Show the picture`, and `Go deeper: <title>`.

**Bold field.** A paragraph that starts with a fixed bold label of `SPEC.json` (`bold_fields`, `fields`), for example `**Decision.**`. A bold field is strict text in a strict role. Any other paragraph that starts with bold text is plain text.

### D1. Predict and Check

```markdown
**Predict 1.** Two teams in different Regions want to use one shelf. Is that possible? <!-- gist-trap -->

**Predict 2.** How many widgets fit on one shelf?
```

Predict prompts have no answers. Exactly one carries `<!-- gist-trap -->`: common sense gets it wrong. One plain line can stand before the prompts to say where the answers are. `**Check <n>.**` asks about the same idea as `**Predict <n>.**` in a new case; its reveal ends with a paragraph `**Predict <n>:**` and one sentence. Every prompt ends with a question mark.

### D2. One-breath definition

The first block of "In one breath" is a `Say it` callout of at most 25 words. Then one bold field:

```markdown
> **Say it:** Amazon Widget Store keeps widgets on shelves. A shelf stores widgets in [one AWS Region](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-scope).

**The problem it solves.** Teams lose widgets when each team keeps its own list.
```

### D3. Words table

```markdown
<!-- table: words -->
| Term | Words that go with it | Plain meaning | Do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|---|
| CIDR block (SY-der blok) | **associate** a CIDR block with a VPC | The address range of a network | subnet mask |
```

Cell 1 is the `term` of `data/terms.json`, or the term and its respelling in parentheses. Cell 2 is one of the term's `collocations` with the partner word, and only that word, in bold. Cells 3 and 4 are free and must not contradict the bank. In `1-fast.md` this table is the definition of the term.

### D4. Comparison table with a deciding question

```markdown
### Shelf or crate

<!-- table: compare -->
| Question | Shelf | Crate |
|---|---|---|
| How many widgets fit? | [500 widgets](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-max-widgets) | [50 widgets](../facts/widget.md#widget-crate-capacity) |

**Deciding question:** Do the widgets stay in one Region, or do they travel?
```

The first header is `Question`. The line after the table is the bold field `**Deciding question:**`, one question.

### D5. Signals table and traps list

```markdown
<!-- table: signals -->
| The stem says | Think | Because | But not if |
|---|---|---|---|
| "send widgets to another Region" | crate | you [cannot move a shelf](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-move) | the stem asks for a copy: copy the shelf |

### Traps

- **Wrong belief:** A shelf is global. **Truth:** A shelf stores widgets in [one AWS Region](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-scope). <!-- trap: sample-trap-shelf-global -->
```

The four headers are exact. Column 1 is a quoted stem phrase. Every row fills "But not if". Each trap is one list item: wrong belief, truth, and a trap id in a comment. Distractors in `questions.json` cite these ids; one trap id can serve several questions with different `concept` values.

Trap ids and the H3 heading texts of "How it works" come from `brief.json` (`traps`, `sections`), so that the writers of prose, questions, cards and tasks can work at the same time. Do not rename them; report a needed change in your result. More trap ids can be defined in `2-deep.md`: an `Exam trap` callout can end with `<!-- trap: <id> -->`.

### D6. Changed recently entry

One callout per change; four labelled lines in this order:

```markdown
> **Changed recently:** (2025-11-19) Maximum number of widgets on a shelf.<br>
> **Old:** [200 widgets](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-max-widgets).<br>
> **Now:** [500 widgets](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-max-widgets).<br>
> **The exam probably expects:** the new value. Prefer an option that is right under both values.<br>
> **Status:** examinable.
```

Each of the first four lines ends with `<br>`. The date is `(YYYY-MM)` or `(YYYY-MM-DD)` and equals `changed.date` of the fact linked in `Now:`. For a new feature write `**Old:** not available.` `Status` is `examinable` or `not examinable yet` (the second for anything generally available after the cut-off date `book.exam_eligibility_cutoff` in `data/book.json`, now 2026-07-15). A `not examinable yet` entry stands in `2-deep.md` directly under the B-list, not in the fast file; `Old:` holds the old value, never the change. With no entry, the section holds one sentence of `grounding.exempt_sentences` (the second when a change waits under the B-list). Example: `CONVENTIONS-2.md` U.

### D7. Recap from memory

`**Recap 1.** <prompt>` followed by a reveal, for each prompt. No summary box anywhere in the book.

### D8. How-it-works subsection

```markdown
### A subnet is public because of one route (4 min)

Body: the problem, a numbered trace, a table when the trace has two or more cases, one "Why" reason; 150 to 400 words in all.

> **Exam signal:** "a subnet with a route to an internet gateway" points to a public subnet, because ...

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: a route that points elsewhere</summary>

Optional edge cases, at most 150 words.

</details>

> **Say it:** The short answer is: one route makes a subnet public. The thing to watch is the public IP address.

**Stop and answer.** Callvale moves a worker into a public subnet. The worker has no public IP address. Can the worker download a package from the internet?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. The route exists, but the worker has no public address.

</details>
```

The heading is an assertion: a full sentence of 5 to 14 words, then the time box. A heading cannot hold a link. A heading with an absolute word or a number with a unit ("An explicit deny always wins") needs at least one fact link for that claim in the body of its subsection. The 150 to 400 words count the paragraphs, lists, tables and callouts of the subsection, not Go deeper blocks, prompts or reveals. Every subsection holds the devices of `CONVENTIONS-2.md` U, with a `Say it` directly before the closing Stop and answer. The Stop and answer applies the text to a new case and never repeats a sentence of the body.

### D9. Go deeper

Edge cases go in a reveal of at most 150 words, inside the subsection, before the Stop and answer:

```markdown
<details>
<summary>Go deeper: empty shelves</summary>

An empty shelf still counts toward the quota.

</details>
```

### D10. Numbers table

```markdown
### A-list

<!-- table: numbers -->
| Number | What it is | Why it decides an answer |
|---|---|---|
| [500 widgets](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-max-widgets) | Maximum on one shelf | More widgets need a second shelf |

### B-list

<!-- table: numbers -->
| Number | What it is |
|---|---|
| [20 shelves per Region](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelves-per-region) | Default quota |
```

Column 1 is always a fact link. A-list numbers are memorised: every A-list row has one `number` flashcard. B-list numbers are recognised; leave the `### B-list` heading out when it has no row.

### D11. Decision record

Five bold fields in this order; `Options.` is followed by a list of 2 to 4 items:

```markdown
**Context.** Callvale stores 300 widgets for one customer in Frankfurt.

**Requirement.** The widgets must stay in one Region with the LEAST operational overhead.

**Options.**

- One shelf in Frankfurt.
- Six crates in Frankfurt.

**Decision.** One shelf in Frankfurt.

**Trade-off.** One shelf is one place to fail. Callvale accepts that at this stage.
```

`Requirement.` is written in exam English. The section has role `example`: the lint asks for no fact link, but link an AWS fact when a field states one. The fields follow the chapter's entry in `STARTUP.md`.

### D12. FAQ quick-fire item

A can-or-cannot question, the short answer, then the verbatim AWS sentence:

```markdown
**FAQ 1.** Can you move a shelf to another Region?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. You [cannot move a shelf](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-move). Copy the shelf.

> "You cannot move a shelf to another Region."
> — [Amazon Widget Store limits](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/widget-store/latest/userguide/limits.html)

</details>
```

**AWS quote block:** a blockquote whose first line is the quote in straight double quotes and whose last line starts with `— [` (an em dash, a space, a link). The quote must be found inside the evidence quote of a verified fact that has the same URL: copy the span exactly, punctuation included; the span holds whole sentences of the evidence quote (F2). The page is any saved AWS page; for FAQ items prefer the service FAQ. The quote is AWS's text: the sentence, word, former-name and term rules do not apply inside it, and its words count toward the file.

### D13. Real-world case box

```markdown
**Tallis Freight, 2025.** [Tallis Freight](../facts/widget.md#widget-case-tallis-freight) moved its widget inventory to Amazon Widget Store.

> "We moved our widget inventory to Amazon Widget Store and stopped running our own storage servers"
> — [Tallis Freight case study](https://aws.amazon.com/solutions/case-studies/tallis-freight/)

**It shows:** a managed service removes operational work.

> **Say it:** Tallis Freight moved its inventory to Widget Store. The team stopped running storage servers.
```

Company and year in bold, at most two sentences with a link to the case-study fact, one AWS quote block of at most 25 words from an AWS-published page, `**It shows:**` with the concept, and a two-sentence `Say it`. The example quote has no final period because the page continues the sentence after it.

### D14. Predict the picture, Spot the bug

The hero diagram is always hidden behind a question:

```markdown
**Predict the picture.** Where does the shelf sit: in a Region, or outside all Regions?

<details>
<summary>Show the picture</summary>

![A Region frame that contains one shelf with widgets](../diagrams/svg/sample-hero.svg)

**Figure.** A shelf sits inside one Region.

1. The team creates a shelf in a Region.
2. The team puts widgets on the shelf.

</details>
```

Spot the bug shows a flawed design or configuration and asks for the flaw: `**Spot the bug.** <design>. What is wrong?`, an optional code block, then a reveal. Use 1 or 2 per deep chapter and 1 per medium chapter, in "How it works" or "Decision record".

### D15. Service card (light family, `2-services.md`)

```markdown
## Amazon Example Service (1 min)
<!-- section: role=card; route=full; minutes=1; service=example-service -->

**Formerly.** Amazon Old Name.

**Say the name.** eg-ZAM-pul.

**What it is.** One sentence.

**Stem signals.**

- "first stem phrase"
- "second stem phrase"

**Do not confuse with.** Other Service: the deciding difference.

**One fact.** At most one linked fact, and only if the exam uses one.

> **Say it:** A sentence of at most 25 words to say in 10 seconds.

**Source.** [Page title](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/...)
```

150 to 220 words (a warning outside the range). `Formerly.`, `Say the name.` and `One fact.` are optional; `What it is.` has one or two sentences. The heading is the official service name without a final part in parentheses and without characters that a heading cannot hold. The former name stands directly after `**Formerly.**`.

### D16. Pattern card (pattern `2-deep.md`)

An H3 with a time box, then six bold fields in order: `**Problem.**` (in exam English), `**Services.**`, `**Cost and effort.**`, `**Choose when.**`, `**Not when.**`, `**Common wrong alternative.**`; then a Stop and answer. A sketch diagram can follow `Problem.`. The H3 is the pattern name (1 to 6 words, for example `Warm standby`), not an assertion. `Problem.` is a scenario, so it is an example block; the other five fields are strict.

## E. Callouts

Syntax: a blockquote that starts with a bold label and a colon inside the bold. At most 60 words.

```markdown
> **Exam trap:** A shelf is not global. A shelf stores widgets in [one AWS Region](../facts/widget.md#widget-shelf-scope).
```

| Label | Use | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| `Key fact` | the one deciding fact of a subsection; optional | writer |
| `Exam signal` | a stem phrase in double quotes, then what it points to; strict after the quoted phrase | writer |
| `Exam trap` | a wrong belief and the truth; can end with a trap id comment (D5) | writer |
| `Exam heuristic` | advice about the exam that AWS does not publish | writer |
| `Changed recently` | an entry as in D6 | writer |
| `Say it` | a sentence to say aloud | writer |
| `Try it` | pointer to a task: one sentence, then `See [3-do.md](3-do.md). <!-- task: <task-id> -->` | writer |
| `Startup example` | the running startup; an example block | writer |
| `Cost`, `Clean up`, `Careful` | task cost, cleanup and safety lines | generated only |

Any other label is a lint error. "Go deeper" is a reveal, not a callout. Limits: 6 callouts in a deep `1-fast.md`, 12 in a deep `2-deep.md` (other templates: `SPEC.json`); only `Key fact`, `Exam trap`, `Try it` and `Startup example` count. Every `Say it` has at most 25 words. Every Try and Lab has exactly one `Try it` callout, in the section that the task's `section` names. The `Startup example` callouts of a chapter hold at most 80 words together.

## F. Fact links

A hard fact is a number, limit, default, price, date, or a "can / cannot", "only / always / never" statement. A hard fact appears only as a Markdown link to the fact canon:

```markdown
A NAT gateway scales [up to 100 Gbps](../facts/vpc.md#vpc-natgw-bandwidth).
```

- Syntax: `[text](../facts/<service>.md#<fact-id>)`. The file stem is the `service` of the fact; the fact id starts with `<service>-`.
- The fact has status `verified` in `data/facts/<service>.jsonl`.
- The link text contains one canonical value string of the fact: one of its `values[].display` strings, compared after `normalize_text` (case does not matter; punctuation does; boundaries and negations: `CONVENTIONS-2.md` F2). Write the value as the canon writes it; do not round, convert or paraphrase it. Facts use digits; a number word ("twelve hours") is never a way around a link. A display without a digit has at most 8 words (F2).
- **When a link is required:** in strict text, every sentence and every table cell that contains a number with a unit (or a counted AWS resource, or a dollar amount) or an absolute word (`only`, `always`, `never`, `cannot`, `must`, `by default`, `up to`, `at most`, `not supported`, and their synonyms, F2) contains at least one fact link. Each hard fact is its own link (F2). A compound such as `read-only` is not an absolute word.
- **Exempt (example blocks):** sections with role `example`, `task`, `worked` or `predict`; prompts; `Startup example` and `Try it` callouts; the "Wrong belief" half of a trap; column 1 of a signals table; the quoted stem phrase at the start of an `Exam signal`; the cells of a `plan` or `log` table; the `Problem.` field of a pattern card and the `Requirement.` field of an architecture page; code blocks and command-like inline code (F2); sentences that end in a question mark (F2). Heading lines, image alt text and bold labels are not checked (headings: D8).
- **Heuristic text** (roles `orient`, `method` and `plan`, `Exam heuristic` callouts, the "Think" and "But not if" columns, the line "The exam probably expects:") can use absolute words without a link. A number with a unit still needs a link, with one exception: a time of the book's own study plan ("20 minutes", "10 seconds") in a sentence that does not speak about the exam itself. Exam rules (length, number of questions, passing score, retake wait) are facts of namespace `exam` and are linked.
- **Not an AWS fact:** a sentence in strict text that states a requirement of a workload ("when the workload must not store keys"), restates a stem condition, or gives a number of her own plan ends with `<!-- no-fact: requirement -->`, `<!-- no-fact: stem -->` or `<!-- no-fact: plan -->`. The lint accepts it and lists it; the claims reviewer checks every use. Never use the marker on an AWS fact.
- Reveals are strict: an answer that states a hard fact links it. Captions and step sentences of diagrams are strict and can hold links (section H).
- The same rule covers these JSON text fields: question `explanation`, `takeaway`, `twin`, option `why`; card `extra`; the `explain` of a break-it step. A number copied from the question's own stem is scenario data and needs no link, and so is the quoted phrase after "If the stem said". Question stems, option texts and spoken scripts never contain links; their item lists the facts in `facts` (how their numbers are checked: `CONVENTIONS-2.md` section J).

**A missing fact.** Never write the sentence from memory. Do both:

1. File a request: `python3 aws-saa-book/tools/saa.py facts request --chapter <id> --service <service> --question "<what the fact must answer>" --where "<file>#<section id>"` (the request queue; exact options in `TOOLS.md`).
2. Leave a marker where the sentence belongs: `<!-- needs-fact: <the same question> -->`.

The marker is a warning while the file is `draft` and an error from `reviewed` on. If the fact cannot be grounded, the sentence is never written. A JSON item that waits for a missing fact is not added: list it in your result as `parked`.

**Another wording of an existing fact.** When no `display` of a fact fits the sentence, do not bend the sentence: run `facts request --chapter <id> --service <service> --fact <fact id> --display "<wanted string>" --where "<file>#<section id>"` and leave `<!-- needs-display: <fact id> "<wanted string>" -->`. The marker has the severity of `needs-fact`.

## G. Tables, code, links, headings, terms

**Tables.** Put a kind comment on the line directly before the header row: `<!-- table: <kind> -->`. Kinds: `compare`, `signals`, `words`, `numbers`, `pairs` (`This | Or this | Deciding difference`), `matrix`, `glance` (`Pattern | Problem | Choose when`), `services` (first header `Service`), `breaks` (`Remove this | What breaks`), `log` (`Date | Question | Cause | Sure | Fix`), `plain`; `plan` and `exam-english` tables are generated. At most 4 columns in `1-fast.md` and 5 elsewhere; at most 15 words in a cell (40 in a `plan` table). At most 16 rows in one table (`matrix` 24, `numbers` 40, `plan` 19). A row count in `SPEC.json` is the sum over the tables of that kind in the section; split a longer list into several tables, each under an H3. No merged cells, no lists or line breaks in cells.

**Code.** Fenced, with a language: `bash`, `json`, `yaml`, `sql`, `python` or `text`. At most 12 lines. No `$` prompt, no account ids, no access keys. A value that she fills in is an uppercase placeholder in angle brackets (`<ACCOUNT_ID>`, `<ROLE_ARN>`), explained next to the block; a `json` policy example can use the documentation account ids `111122223333` and `444455556666`. Commands run in AWS CloudShell. Task commands are never typed into prose; they live in `tasks.json`.

**Links.**

| Target | Syntax |
|---|---|
| same file | `[text](#signals-and-traps-4-min)` |
| same chapter | `[text](2-deep.md#a-subnet-is-public-because-of-one-route-4-min)` |
| other chapter | `[text](../10-vpc/1-fast.md#choose-it-or-not-5-min)` |
| glossary | `[idempotent](../glossary.md#idempotent)` |
| fact | section F |
| AWS page | only inside an AWS quote block or the `Source.` field of a service card; hosts `docs.aws.amazon.com`, `aws.amazon.com`, `d1.awsstatic.com`, `repost.aws` |

No bare URLs. Link to a section only after it exists; lint resolves every anchor. To point to a chapter that is not written yet (IAM points to SCPs in chapter 04), write no link: put `<!-- xref: <chapter id> <topic words> -->` after the sentence. The lint lists these markers as warnings; the whole-book review turns each into a real link. A link to a generated file of the chapter (`3-do.md`, `4-say.md`, `5-quiz.md`) carries no anchor.

**Headings.** One H1. H2 and H3 only below it. H2 and H3 text uses letters, digits, spaces and hyphens, plus the time box ` (N min)` at the end. A time box is required on every H2 and on every H3 inside a `how` section. The Markdown anchor is the GitHub anchor of the full text: lowercase, drop every character that is not a letter, digit, space or hyphen, spaces to hyphens (`## Check (2 min)` gives `#check-2-min`). The tool's stable section id is the same anchor without the time box (`check`); JSON items use section ids (`"section": "2-deep.md#a-subnet-is-public-because-of-one-route"`).

**Glossary terms.** Every word that is bold as a definition, and every row of a words table, is a term of `data/terms.json`, spelled as its `term` field. Other AWS nouns are free. The build links the first use in each H2 to the glossary; add nothing. Make a term bold where it is defined, and give the respelling at first use: `**idempotent** (eye-dem-POH-tent)`. A `banned` variant is a lint error; an `avoid` entry is a variant that is wrong only in the context it names. The short forms that need the full term first are the `short` values of the bank; official service names and the H1 are exempt. The brief lists the terms of a chapter, and the orchestrator adds them to the bank before the writers start. For a term that is still missing, write `<!-- needs-term: <term> -->` at its first use and list it in your result as `term_requests` (a warning in `draft`, an error from `reviewed`); do not edit `terms.json`.

**Raw HTML.** Only `details`, `summary`, `br`, `sub`, `sup`, `kbd` and comments.

## H. Diagram embeds

```markdown
![Two subnets in one VPC with a NAT gateway in the public subnet](../diagrams/svg/vpc-nat-path.svg)

**Figure.** A private instance reaches the internet through the NAT gateway.

1. The instance sends the request to the NAT gateway.
2. The NAT gateway sends the request to the internet gateway.
```

- The image is alone in its paragraph. The alt text describes the picture in at least 20 characters.
- Diagram id: `<slug>-<name>`, kebab-case; the chapter's hero diagram is `<slug>-hero`. The source spec is `diagrams/src/<diagram-id>.json` (format: `_build/style/TOOLS-diagram.md`).
- The caption is the next paragraph: `**Figure.**` and one claim sentence of at most 24 words. A claim says what is true, not what the picture is ("A shelf sits inside one Region", not "Shelf architecture").
- If the spec has numbered steps, an ordered list follows the caption with one sentence per step, in the same order.
- **One text, two places.** `brief.json` lists each diagram of the chapter with `id`, `kind`, `claim`, `alt` and the `say` sentence of each step. The diagram agent writes the spec from it; the prose writer copies the same text. Alt text = spec `alt`, caption = spec `claim`, step sentences = spec `say`, compared after links are replaced by their text. A caption or step that states a hard fact carries the fact link in the Markdown.
- The hero diagram sits inside "Predict the picture" (D14) and is of kind `arch` or `sequence` (the kinds that have steps). A draft can be linted before the SVG exists.
