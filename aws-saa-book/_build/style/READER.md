# The reader

One person: Tatia. Write for her, and judge every page as she would read it.

**Who she is.** AWS Startup Solutions Architect in San Francisco since late July 2026. Before that an AI/ML researcher and engineer (LLMs, evaluation, jailbreak defence). Strong technically; newer to cloud infrastructure detail. Passed AWS Cloud Practitioner. First language Georgian; English is her second language.

**What she must do.** Pass SAA-C03 by Oct 31, 2026 (date not booked; about 40 study hours; five to six Tutorials Dojo mocks). Answer customer technical questions out loud to colleagues in the week of Oct 13. Explain AWS services to startup founders every working day after that.

**What she says about herself** (her own notes):
- She forgets words, names and terminology, especially under stress.
- She struggles to articulate clearly when nervous.
- Long LLM-generated text intimidates her and she often does not finish it.
- She acts before she reads, and trusts the gist she gets from one glance.
- She uses Anki daily and wants to read 10 pages a day.

**Diagnostic, 2026-09-18** (65 questions, finished in 51 of 130 minutes): 69% overall. Secure 45% (9/20), Resilient 71%, High-Performing 80%, Cost-Optimized 92%. Right but not sure: EBS volume types, EFS vs FSx, Aurora Serverless, DynamoDB global tables, S3 tiering. Strong: pricing models and cost tools, Lambda, API Gateway, Fargate.

**What she asked for this book:** do not stop midway; go deeper than a 5,700-word chapter; make it easier to read (shorter sections, real diagrams); more practice (questions, spoken answers, hands-on tasks); vocabulary cards in English only.

**What this means for writing.**
- A file she can finish in 15 to 20 minutes gets opened. Put the short version first, always.
- Headings, tables and captions must give a correct gist on their own; questions must test what the gist misses.
- Depth lives in short subsections with a time box and a Stop-and-answer, not in long paragraphs.
- Every chapter gives her sentences she can say: a one-breath definition, collocations ("assume a role"), and model answers written for speech.
- She reads fast and rushes questions. Train her to find the constraint in the stem before looking at the options.
- She works at AWS. A wrong fact is worse than a missing one.
- Tone: calm, direct, adult, second person. No cheerleading, no "simply", no "just".

**Style she already uses** (earlier study packs): a guide plus a cheat sheet plus a talk track; headings such as "Say it in one breath", "What it really is", "Use it when".
